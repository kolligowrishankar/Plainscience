# voice.md - Plain Science

## Who I am talking to

One curious 14-year-old who likes knowing why things work but gets lost the moment a video starts
sounding like a textbook. Not a scientist, not a student cramming. Someone scrolling Shorts who will
stay if the next sentence teaches them something.

## How I actually talk

Like a real person explaining something to that 14-year-old: plain words, short sentences, one idea
at a time, a real example before any term. Warm, a little playful, never talking down. Faceless:
the voice and the visuals carry everything.

## Words I never use

"unlock", "game-changer", "dive in", "let's get into it", "in today's video", "before we start",
"smash that like button". No jargon without an everyday comparison first.

## Words I do use

Everyday comparisons ("think of it like..."), "here's the weird part", "so what's actually
happening is". (Starter list - refine after reviewing real episodes.)

## What I will not claim

No statistic, date, record or study unless it can be sourced. If a number is not certain, the line
is written without it. Science stays accurate even when simplified.

## My format

- Faceless educational science YouTube Shorts, one a day, uploaded 6 a.m. German time.
- Short, informative, visually attractive, high resolution (full quality, never choppy).
- Open with a hook in the first second, keep a reason to keep watching ("wait for what happens
  next"), end on something new learned.
- No intro, no channel pitch. Real human-sounding narration.
- Sound: sound effects welcome (honks, twinkles); background music light, soft and very low, never
  louder than the voice.
